01 / About

Research-minded. Hardware-first.

I am a postdoctoral researcher at the University of Illinois Chicago, where I work at the intersection of computer architecture, digital and mixed-signal circuits, and machine learning. I received my Ph.D. in Electrical and Computer Engineering from UIC in 2025, advised by Dr. Arman Roohi.

My work connects device-level constraints with real applications: moving computation closer to pixels and memory, designing accelerators that can operate with limited energy, and developing modeling tools that help predict performance, power, and area before implementation.

Alongside my research, I build and verify RTL designs in SystemVerilog, prototype on Xilinx FPGAs, and develop C/C++ and Python software to test hardware and evaluate new architectures.

Featured FPGA project

RISC-V ML Core: RV32IM + custom INT8 instructions

Designed single-cycle and five-stage pipelined RISC-V CPUs from scratch in SystemVerilog, with forwarding, hazard handling, block RAM, iterative division, and a four-lane INT8 dot-product extension.

Both cores pass 48 official RV32I/RV32M tests. The pipelined implementation meets timing at 65 MHz on a PYNQ-Z2; custom instructions accelerate an integer-only 784 → 32 → 10 MNIST classifier.
